# Brand-kit image direction

First check for a real image-generation tool. If none is available, say so and stop; do not invent a call, file, or artifact. If the user supplied references, study their rhythm and quality, not their marks or composition.

Create a coherent identity board, not a random moodboard. Infer the category, audience, promise, metaphor, and what the brand should avoid. Reduce the metaphor into one simple mark; use at most two construction ideas (monogram, product action, metaphor fusion, negative space, or geometry). Never copy a known logo or imply legal ownership/clearance.

## Board direction

Unless the brief says otherwise, make one 3×3 or 2×3 board with generous gutters and sparse, readable text. Give it a clear rhythm: logo/wordmark, construction or symbol logic, digital application, essence/tagline, palette, type, physical application, image direction, and one system detail. Omit panels that do not help the concept. Use one dominant palette, one recurring accent, and a restrained presentation canvas. Keep mockups believable and partial; they are identity applications, not fake product demos.

Use the supplied category and references to choose light/dark, editorial/technical, calm/expressive, and image treatment. Do not force the default dark deck, gradient, texture, or grid. Keep text to the brand name, one short tagline, a few labels, and readable UI fragments. Avoid lorem ipsum, tiny fake copy, stock office scenes, generic AI glows, rainbow palettes, random icons, copied marks, and decorative clutter.

## Optional direction bank

Choose the category's real promise before choosing a motif. Useful starting points include: developer tools—build/precision/frame; assistants—delegation/signal/path; security—boundary/watch/protected core; productivity—focus/path/check; voice—wave/pulse/speech; luxury/editorial—ritual/seal/material; climate or travel—horizon/route/compass. Applications can be a browser or terminal crop, app icon, stationery, badge, packaging, poster, label, or restrained pattern. Pick the few that prove the system; do not fill every panel.

Return the generated image(s) and a short concept label only. State when an image is a concept and that trademark research is separate.
